## What is the waterfall model?

The Waterfall model is a sequential, linear approach to project management, where each phase (requirements gathering, design, development, testing, and deployment) must be completed before moving on to the next. It usually needs predefined set of user requirements that don't change

## What is kanban
Kanban, a methodology emphasising visualising work and limiting work in progress (WIP), is particularly beneficial in environments focusing on bug fixes and testing.

By visualising tasks on a Kanban board and setting WIP limits, data engineering teams can effectively manage complex workflows, prioritise tasks, and enhance project visibility. 

This approach ensures that teams can focus on completing tasks efficiently while maintaining high-quality standards in bug fixing and testing processes.

## What is scrum
Scrum, a widely adopted Agile framework, emphasises efficient collaboration and streamlined workflows through specific roles and ceremonies. 

One of its core ceremonies, the daily standup, is designed to be short and focused, with team members sharing what they've accomplished, what they're working on next, and any obstacles they're facing. 

The scrum master plays a crucial role in facilitating these standups, ensuring that discussions stay on track, distractions are minimised, and team members adhere to the agenda.




## What is the Lean Approach?

Lean is a continuous improvement methodology focused on eliminating waste and optimising processes. 

## What is the Six Sigma Approach
Six Sigma, on the other hand, is a data-driven approach aimed at improving quality by reducing variability and defects.

## What is the primary objective of using the "5 Whys" technique?

To identify the root cause of the defects. The basic concept is to ask “why?” five times to dig deep into the root of a problem. The logic behind it is that in the first few questions you will find one of the causes of the problem, and by the 5th question you will see the process failure behind that problem. 


## Topic 3 Apply Task 1:

At St. James’s Place (SJP), a data-driven culture means using accurate and reliable data to support business decisions and improve the services provided to clients. Key stakeholders include the Product Owner, Scrum Master, Data Engineers, Data Analysts, Data Acquisition Analysts and Data Governance Analysts. They need to work together to understand business requirements, collect data from relevant sources, maintain data quality and ensure client information is handled securely. Regular meetings, backlog refinement and feedback from stakeholders can help keep data projects aligned with business objectives. Agile, Lean and Six Sigma can support this by delivering improvements in stages, reducing unnecessary manual work and identifying data-quality problems. Success could be measured through fewer data errors, faster processing times and more reliable data for client reporting.


## Topic 3 Apply Task 2:

For my second task, I would use a project focused on building a reliable data pipeline to prepare client-related data for reporting at St. James’s Place (SJP). The overall epic would be to make sure data is collected, checked and prepared accurately so that authorised employees can use it for reporting. I would work with the Data Acquisition Analyst to identify data sources, the Data Governance Analyst to check data is handled appropriately, and the Data Analyst to understand reporting requirements. The Product Owner would prioritise the work, while the Scrum Master would help the team work effectively.

Example user stories using the template “As a…, I need…, in order to…” would include: “As a Data Engineer, I need to validate incoming data in order to identify errors before it is used for reporting” and “As a Data Analyst, I need access to accurate, prepared data in order to produce reliable reports.” Acceptance criteria for the first story could include identifying missing values and duplicate records, flagging invalid data and recording any errors found. For the second story, the data must be accurate, available in the agreed format and accessible only to authorised users. I would prioritise collecting the data first, then validating and preparing it, before making it available for analysis. This would help the team deliver the project in manageable stages while maintaining data quality and protecting client information.
